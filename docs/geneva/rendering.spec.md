# G4 — Visual language and rendering
Issue #62; dependent on G1–G3. Preserve existing Three.js and MIT AEROVIEW facade reuse with attribution. No new engine or wholesale dependency migration unless justified.

## Art direction
Contemporary retro-futurist skyline within Geneva urban morphology: golden-hour amber lighting, restrained haze, blue-green lake and differentiated Arve water, warm stone and neutral civic paving, trees and larger parks, continuous horizon. Water surface shading should make shorelines readable without realistic fluid simulation. Lakefront quays with stone edge, promenades, bridges with parapets and approach roads; no fake water crossings. Use subtle contact shading, restrained materials, coherent visual scale. Avoid identical compound pads and repeating road markings everywhere.
Buildings align to actual street frontage; urban block perimeter, articulated roofs, courtyards, varied setbacks and selectively clustered towers. Decorative low-rise infill is optional, visibly neutral and non-selectable. No fabricated graph nodes or prestige based on architectural quality. Four approved silhouettes: structural, layered/modular, precise, articulated; construction scaffold denotes incomplete classification, not unfinished mathematics. Height monotonic in represented source volume with bounds, not role or library. Same subject palette across banks; neutral terrain; labels and silhouettes provide non-colour cues. Maintain contrast and evidence labels. Do not conceal actual source entities behind decorative meshes.

## Rendering architecture
Pure geographic/placement data consumed by Three.js layers: terrain/water, civic street mesh, bridges/props, mathematical buildings, semantic dependency channels, labels/picking. Reusable geometries, texture atlases/shared materials, instancing for repeated props and simple proxies. LOD tiers: entire city coarse proxies + sector labels; intermediate subject labels and block massing; close complex detailed facades and inspector. Preserve pick IDs and selection when changing LOD. No per-window mesh explosion; avoid costly full-scene shadows. Dispose geometries/materials/textures on teardown; do not recreate full geography on subject selection. Record third-party assets, source URL, exact revision and licence.
Dependencies: pink uses and amber used-by sky lines, cyan local Mathlib ground conduits; distinct from streets and bridges. Cross-bank edges follow recorded source evidence; visual routing may follow bridge corridor but cannot add graph edges or imply that no path exists elsewhere. Bound visible sky links and show omitted counts/exact drilldown. Stations remain one local aggregate per complex, empty state explicit. Graph and declaration evidence handled under #49/#51/#52 and #4/#8/#35.

## Night Street reference and opening composition
Reference: [StarKnightt/night-street](https://github.com/StarKnightt/night-street/tree/15685fe5011d4645e27b68e68547912ae7f0f327), MIT. Reviewed src/world/block.ts, src/world/bake.ts, src/scene/Buildings.tsx and Lighting.tsx. Borrow the principles of variable street frontage, material-batched geometry, procedural PBR textures and a limited real-light budget; do not transplant the React/Next street renderer, fixed low-rise architecture or expensive street-level shaders wholesale. No Night Street source or assets copied in this change. Existing licensed AEROVIEW facades are reused. User-approved direction is atomic-age skyscraper futurism: stepped towers, circular observation decks, fins, metallic crowns and restrained emissive trim. North-up 3D orthographic composition opens by default, flat plan remains available, with reduced-motion-friendly optional entrance animation. Illustrative G1 massing is not semantic geometry.

Source reviewed: GeneratorItem_CityBlock.js, Generator.js, AssetManager.js, index.js.
