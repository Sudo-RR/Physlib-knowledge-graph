# Geneva adaptive geometry and performance gate

The previous verified Chromium run (45654164571, Physlib 63250d48dc796f669f04043fcd5c2a1ce36e12e4) reported 981,557 triangles, 567 draw calls and zero idle redraws at the overview. Software WebGL evidence is not hardware FPS evidence.

Adaptive geometry is the default production setting; the architectural study defaults to full detail. Full detail is available in the Geometry control independently of the existing DPR Quality control. Adaptive rendering batches original geometry and simpler textured role silhouettes by library bank and subject. Near the camera focus, a quality-dependent orthographic screen-space scale selects full geometry; far districts retain simple silhouettes. The selected district has a lower threshold at focus. The layout, building and complex IDs, picking, dependencies, review markers and flat map are unchanged. Civic trees and round infrastructure use lower segment counts at overview; roads and infill remain. No source module or building is omitted by LOD. The original high-detail models stay available; both LOD representations are allocated, so GPU memory and startup cost are not guaranteed to decrease. The simplification is district-level, not per-instance LOD. Detailed scenery is switched globally by projected scale.

Quality thresholds (CSS pixels/world unit): low 2.5, medium 1.65, high 1.15; selected district threshold multiplied by 0.75. Nearby means distance from the focus point to the district's axis-aligned bounds is within 1.1 times half the camera projection diagonal. Full detail bypasses this policy. No forced FPS or adaptive resolution controller is introduced.

The production browser gate compares adaptive and full detail at identical camera, source revision and viewport. Require at least 25% fewer triangles, no increase in draw calls, identical metrics on restoring adaptive mode, no idle redraw regression, and both detailed and simplified geometry at focus. Capture both overview modes and the focused view. Record triangles, draw calls, pixel ratio, canvas size, geometry/texture counts, JS CPU render submission time and six navigation samples. CPU submission and browser automation wall time are not GPU frame time; no hardware FPS claim or timing gate. A separate hardware benchmark at fixed resolution and DPR remains necessary. The quality thresholds and relative budgets are provisional engineering choices, subject to visual and hardware evaluation.
